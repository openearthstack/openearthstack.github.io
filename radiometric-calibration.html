Radiometric Calibration.

SAR reports backscatter as one of three normalised quantities. Picking and reporting the right one affects every downstream model.

The three naughts

Beta naught (β⁰)
Radar brightness. Reflectivity per unit slant-range area. The raw output of calibration, before geometric normalisation.
Sigma naught (σ⁰)
Normalised radar cross-section per unit ground area. Accounts for incidence angle on a flat Earth. Standard for most land and maritime workloads.
Gamma naught (γ⁰)
Normalised by area perpendicular to the look direction. Less sensitive to local incidence angle, which makes it the preferred quantity after terrain correction in hilly terrain.

Relationships

Given local incidence angle θ:

  • σ⁰ = β⁰ · sin(θ)
  • γ⁰ = σ⁰ / cos(θ)

On flat terrain with constant incidence the three differ only by a scaling factor. In mountainous terrain, the difference between σ⁰ and γ⁰ can be large and affects classification significantly.

Which to report

  • Flat terrain, land or sea: σ⁰ in dB.
  • Mountainous terrain, after RTC: γ⁰ in dB.
  • Raw pre-geometry analyses: β⁰, but rarely consumed directly downstream.

Units

Calibrated SAR backscatter is dimensionless (a power ratio). Values are typically reported in decibels: dB = 10 · log10(σ⁰). Land sigma0 is usually between −30 dB (dark, smooth) and +5 dB (bright corner reflectors). Open calm water can drop to −25 dB or below.

Always document which naught you report, in which unit, and after which preprocessing steps. Models trained on σ⁰-dB do not transfer cleanly to γ⁰-dB.
