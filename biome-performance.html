Per-Biome Model Performance.

A global average accuracy tells you nothing about whether a SAR model will work where you operate. Per-biome performance does.

Tropical

Dense broadleaf canopy attenuates the SAR signal and changes seasonally with phenology. Moist soil backscatter swings significantly with rainfall. Models trained on temperate data often underperform badly here; tropical-specific benchmarks are essential. SAR's value is highest in the tropics because optical sensors fail under persistent cloud.

Temperate

Mixed land cover, strong seasonality, broad availability of labelled training data. Most foundation models perform best here because the training distribution is dominated by temperate imagery. Do not assume these numbers generalise.

Arid

Bare surfaces (sand, gravel, playa) produce very low backscatter. Signal-to-noise ratio is poor. Small variations in moisture or vegetation become relatively large in the backscatter domain. Models trained on wetter regions can misclassify dunes as water.

Boreal

Needle-leaf forests, frequent snow cover, persistent low sun angle in optical products. SAR sees through the dark months; models need to handle frozen versus thawed states, snow accumulation, and wetland cover.

Arctic and cryosphere

Sea ice, glaciers, permafrost. HH polarisation is preferred where available. Deformation from thaw subsidence is a major use case. Models trained without cryosphere data will make confident nonsense here.

A headline "90% accuracy" is meaningless without the worst per-biome number. Ask any model vendor for the per-biome breakdown before you trust it in your deployment region.
