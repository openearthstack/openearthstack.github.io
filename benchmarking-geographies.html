Benchmarking Across Geographies.

Most public SAR benchmarks systematically under-sample the places where operational deployments actually happen. Designing a better benchmark is not optional.

The failure mode

Public benchmarks are dominated by regions with dense labelled data: Europe, North America, parts of East Asia. Models trained and evaluated on these regions transfer poorly to the Sahel, the Amazon, Central Asia, and the Pacific. The benchmark score looks good; the field deployment fails.

Design principles

  1. Sample geographically. Include biomes and continents in proportion to where the model will be deployed, not where labels are easy to collect.
  2. Test on held-out geographies. A random-scene split is not a generalisation test; a held-out continent or biome is.
  3. Fix the split publicly. Publish the exact train / val / test geographies so others can reproduce without peeking.
  4. Document label provenance. Human-annotated? Derived from another sensor? Known-wrong anywhere? All of this matters.
  5. Report worst-case metrics. The mean matters less than the per-region minimum.
  6. Cover ascending and descending orbits. They differ physically; a model good on one can fail on the other.

Public efforts worth knowing

  • EuroSAT, BigEarthNet, xView2: widely used, regionally biased.
  • Sen1Floods11: global flood benchmark with geographic splits.
  • SpaceNet 6: SAR-optical multimodal building footprint benchmark.
  • DFC (Data Fusion Contest) series: annual community benchmarks with rotating themes.
Publish a benchmark that can embarrass your own model in regions you care about. If it can only say you did well, it cannot tell you when you will fail.
