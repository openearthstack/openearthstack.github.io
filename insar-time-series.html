How to Run an InSAR Time Series.

InSAR measures phase differences between SAR acquisitions to produce mm-scale deformation maps. PSI and SBAS are the two dominant time-series techniques.

The principle

Each SLC pixel carries a phase proportional to the sensor-to-target distance. Two precisely co-registered SLCs of the same area differ in phase by flat-earth, topographic, atmospheric, noise, and any actual surface movement. Remove the first three and you recover line-of-sight displacement with wavelength-scale accuracy — at C-band that is roughly 5.6 cm per 2π, so mm-level with careful processing.

PSI — Persistent Scatterer Interferometry

Works on stable point-like scatterers (buildings, rock outcrops, infrastructure) that remain coherent over years.

  1. Stack of SLCs, one chosen as the master.
  2. Form interferograms against the master; remove topographic phase with a DEM.
  3. Identify pixels with temporally stable amplitude (Amplitude Dispersion Index).
  4. Estimate linear velocity and DEM error per PS point; solve for residual atmosphere.
  5. Output: per-point velocity (mm/yr) and displacement time series.

Best in urban, exposed-rock, and infrastructure-rich settings.

SBAS — Small Baseline Subset

Uses only interferogram pairs with short temporal and geometric baselines to maximise coherence on distributed scatterers (soil, sparse vegetation). Pairs are combined through least-squares or SVD inversion. Better than PSI for rural, agricultural, and natural terrain.

Tooling

Open implementations: GMTSAR, ISCE2, StaMPS (PSI), MintPy (post-processing), SNAPHU (phase unwrapping). Commercial: GAMMA, SARPROZ. Cloud: Hyp3 (ASF) for on-demand interferograms.

Expect noise on the order of 1–3 mm/yr in well-behaved stacks of 25+ scenes. Sentinel-1's 12-day revisit yields roughly 30 acquisitions per year over most of the world.
