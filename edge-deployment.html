How to Deploy Models at the Edge.

Taking a SAR model from a training cluster to a laptop in a field office, without giving up accuracy or reliability.

Why the edge

Many SAR deployment contexts have no reliable cloud connection: ship decks, air-gapped ops centres, field offices in low-connectivity regions, sovereign deployments with data-residency constraints. Shipping models that run locally is the difference between a product that works in those contexts and one that does not.

Quantisation

Most SAR backbones are trained in FP32. Converting to FP16 roughly halves memory with no measurable accuracy loss. INT8 cuts memory by 4× and usually keeps top-line accuracy within 1–2 points if calibrated correctly. Post-training quantisation is enough for most inference workloads; quantisation-aware training helps for borderline cases.

Pruning

Structured pruning (removing whole channels or heads) is compatible with standard runtimes; unstructured pruning needs sparse-kernel support. Pruning 30–50% of parameters is realistic for vision backbones at modest accuracy cost.

Distillation

When the target hardware is tight (handheld, embedded SoC), train a smaller student model against a larger teacher's outputs. Well-designed distillation usually matches the teacher within a few percentage points at a fraction of the compute.

Runtimes

  • ONNX Runtime: cross-platform, CPU-first, broad hardware acceleration.
  • TensorRT: best throughput on NVIDIA GPUs.
  • OpenVINO: strong on Intel CPUs and integrated GPUs.
  • ggml / gguf: for very tight CPU-only environments.

Pipeline compression

The model is often the smaller part. Preprocessing (orbit application, calibration, terrain correction) can dwarf model inference in both compute and I/O. Fuse preprocessing into the inference graph where possible, cache DEMs for the operating area, and stream tiles rather than loading full scenes.

OpenEarthStack's reference inference pipeline fits under 8 GB RAM and processes a Sentinel-1 IW scene end-to-end in under 20 seconds on commodity CPUs.
