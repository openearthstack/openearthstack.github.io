SAR Product Types.

Sentinel-1 processes raw radar returns into several distinct product levels. Choosing the right one is usually the first decision in any SAR pipeline.

Level 0 — Raw

Unfocused raw radar echoes. Only relevant to mission processors and specialised research. Most users never touch L0.

Level 1 — SLC (Single Look Complex)

Focused, geo-referenced in slant range, with both amplitude and phase preserved as complex numbers. The phase is what makes InSAR possible. SLC scenes are large (roughly 8 GB for a Sentinel-1 IW scene) and require careful co-registration before stacking.

Level 1 — GRD (Ground Range Detected)

Amplitude-only product, multi-looked and projected to ground range. Easier to work with than SLC but phase is discarded, so no InSAR. Standard input for flood mapping, backscatter classification, and most land-cover applications. Available at High Resolution (HR) or Medium Resolution (MR).

Level 2 — OCN (Ocean)

Ocean geophysical products derived from WV or EW mode: wind field, wave spectra, surface currents. Used by maritime agencies and climate services.

Derived — RTC (Radiometrically Terrain Corrected)

Not an ESA-native product. Generated by downstream processors (ASF, Microsoft Planetary Computer, others) from GRD or SLC. Backscatter is normalised for terrain-induced distortions using a DEM, producing imagery ready for direct analysis.

Derived — ARD (Analysis Ready Data)

A catch-all for preprocessed, calibrated, terrain-corrected, cloud-optimised outputs. CEOS publishes an ARD specification for SAR; many teams (OpenEarthStack included) ship pipelines that conform to it.

Typical ingestion pattern: download GRD or RTC from a public archive, convert to COG, catalogue as STAC. If you need InSAR, start with SLC and plan for more storage and compute.
