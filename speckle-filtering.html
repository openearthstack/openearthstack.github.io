Speckle Filtering Compared.

Speckle is intrinsic to coherent imaging. A handful of filters cover most SAR workflows; each has its trade-offs.

What speckle is

Constructive and destructive interference of the coherent radar signal within each resolution cell produces pixel-to-pixel intensity variations even over homogeneous surfaces. It is multiplicative noise, not additive, and follows (for multi-look intensity) a gamma distribution. Gaussian denoisers designed for optical imagery handle it poorly.

Classical filters

Lee (1980)
Adaptive local mean filter. Preserves edges reasonably well; over-smooths in textured areas.
Frost (1982)
Exponentially weighted local filter. Similar performance to Lee, slightly better detail preservation in some cases.
Gamma-MAP
Bayesian MAP estimate assuming gamma-distributed reflectivity. Good radiometric consistency; tends to smooth edges.
Refined Lee
Lee filter with directional edge-preserving kernels. De facto standard for visual-quality single-image SAR filtering.

Multi-temporal filters

Where you have a stack of scenes, multi-temporal despeckling outperforms any single-image filter.

  • Temporal averaging after co-registration.
  • SAR2SAR and other deep-learning approaches trained on multi-temporal stacks.
  • Quegan filter for balanced multi-temporal despeckling.

When to skip filtering

Modern ML models trained on speckled imagery often do not need input despeckling — they learn through speckle. Filtering can even hurt downstream accuracy by removing real high-frequency detail. Benchmark both options on your task before defaulting to any single filter.

Rule of thumb: filter when the downstream consumer is human (visual interpretation) or a model trained on filtered inputs. Skip when the downstream consumer is a model trained on raw multi-look data.
