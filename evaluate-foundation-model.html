How to Evaluate a SAR Foundation Model.

A SAR foundation model that scores well on a global benchmark can still fail in your deployment context. Here is how to evaluate one before committing.

Split the benchmark the right way

Random scene-split is misleading. Models that look strong on random splits often fail on geographic out-of-distribution data. Split by:

  • Biome (tropical, temperate, arid, boreal, arctic).
  • Land cover class for supervised tasks.
  • Acquisition geometry (ascending vs descending, relative orbit).
  • Season where vegetation phenology matters.

Report per-context metrics

Global averages hide everything. Publish mean IoU, F1, or mAP broken down by each of the splits above. The worst-case region is usually the decisive number for operational deployment.

Test label provenance

Where did the benchmark labels come from? Human-annotated? Derived from optical imagery (with its cloud bias)? Known-wrong in certain regions? A model that scores 0.9 against biased labels is not necessarily a better model.

Calibration

Compare predicted confidence against empirical accuracy. A miscalibrated model that returns 0.99 on obvious failures is dangerous in production regardless of its headline score.

Failure-mode analysis

Look at the worst 100 predictions. Cluster them. Common clusters for SAR models: urban floods, windy water, radar shadow in steep terrain, agricultural fields in senescence. A model that ships with documented failure modes is easier to trust than one that hides them.

If a benchmark does not break results down by biome, geography, or season, treat the headline score as marketing. Ask the authors for the per-context numbers before you build on it.
