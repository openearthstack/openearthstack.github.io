How to Preprocess Sentinel-1.

The standard pipeline from a raw Sentinel-1 scene to an analysis-ready, terrain-corrected backscatter product.

Input

A Sentinel-1 IW GRD scene (amplitude, already multi-looked and projected to ground range) or an IW SLC scene (complex, pre-focused). GRD is the common starting point for backscatter analysis. SLC is required for InSAR.

Pipeline

  1. Apply precise orbit file. ESA publishes restituted (within hours) and precise (within about 20 days) orbit files. Precise yields better terrain correction.
  2. Thermal noise removal. Subtract the noise floor. Especially important for cross-polarised channels over dark surfaces.
  3. Border noise removal. GRD scenes have elevated noise at swath edges. Mask or attenuate.
  4. Radiometric calibration. Convert digital numbers to sigma naught (or beta / gamma naught) using the calibration LUTs in the product.
  5. Speckle filtering (optional). Skip if you plan to use multi-temporal averaging or trust your model to learn through speckle.
  6. Terrain correction. Range-Doppler orthorectification with a DEM (SRTM or Copernicus DEM). Produces sigma0 or gamma0 on a regular map grid.
  7. Conversion to dB (optional). Log-scale for modelling or display.
  8. Output. Write as Cloud Optimized GeoTIFF and catalogue as STAC Item.

Tools

ESA SNAP (desktop), sentineleof and pyroSAR (Python), asf_tools (ASF), and commercial processors all implement this chain. OpenEarthStack wraps it in a documented, reproducible pipeline.

Most production downstream models are trained on gamma0-RTC in dB. If you are consuming a third-party model, confirm the exact preprocessing it expects before feeding raw scenes to it.
