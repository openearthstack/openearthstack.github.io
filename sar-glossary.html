SAR Glossary.

The core vocabulary of synthetic aperture radar, defined once, for quick reference during production work.

Core concepts

SAR
Synthetic Aperture Radar. Active radar imaging that synthesises a large virtual antenna from a moving platform.
Backscatter
Portion of the radar signal reflected back to the sensor. Usually reported as sigma naught in dB.
Speckle
Salt-and-pepper noise inherent to coherent imaging. Multiplicative, not additive.
Coherence
Phase similarity between two SAR acquisitions. Ranges 0–1. Critical for InSAR.
Incidence angle
Angle between the radar beam and the vertical at the target. Affects backscatter intensity.
Slant range
Line-of-sight distance from sensor to target.
Ground range
Horizontal distance from nadir to target on the Earth surface.
Azimuth
Along-track direction (flight direction of the platform).
Multilooking
Averaging neighbouring pixels to reduce speckle at the cost of resolution.

Polarimetry

VV
Vertical transmit, vertical receive. Dominant for most land surfaces.
VH
Vertical transmit, horizontal receive. Cross-polarised; sensitive to volume scattering in vegetation.
HH
Horizontal transmit, horizontal receive. Used for ice, snow, some flood-mapping contexts.
HV
Horizontal transmit, vertical receive. Cross-polarised; similar sensitivity to VH.
Dual-pol
Two channels (usually VV+VH). Standard for Sentinel-1 IW mode.
Quad-pol
All four channels. Information-rich but narrow swath.

Interferometry

InSAR
Interferometric SAR. Uses phase differences between two SLC acquisitions to measure line-of-sight displacement or topography.
Interferogram
Phase difference image between two co-registered SLCs.
PSI
Persistent Scatterer Interferometry. Uses stable point scatterers to produce mm-accuracy deformation time series.
SBAS
Small Baseline Subset. Combines small-baseline interferograms for distributed-scatterer time series.
Baseline
Geometric separation between two SAR acquisitions. Small baselines improve coherence.

Radiometric quantities

Beta naught (β⁰)
Radar brightness per unit slant-range area.
Sigma naught (σ⁰)
Normalised radar cross-section per unit ground area. Standard reporting.
Gamma naught (γ⁰)
Normalised by area perpendicular to the look direction. Slope-independent.
