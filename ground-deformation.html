How to Monitor Ground Deformation.

SAR interferometry is the only remote-sensing technique that routinely measures mm-scale surface movement at regional scale. The deployment question is choosing the right workflow for the terrain.

Applications

  • Subsidence: groundwater extraction, mining, permafrost thaw.
  • Slope instability: landslides, open-pit mines, tailings dams.
  • Infrastructure: bridges, dams, transport corridors, pipelines.
  • Volcanoes and tectonics: inflation, rifting, co-seismic displacement.

Urban and infrastructure: PSI

Persistent Scatterer Interferometry excels where many pixels remain coherent over years: cities, industrial sites, rock faces. It produces per-point velocities with mm-level precision, but only where stable scatterers exist.

Natural terrain: SBAS

Small Baseline Subset handles vegetated and agricultural land better by combining short-baseline interferograms. It produces gridded velocity fields rather than point clouds, at the cost of coarser spatial resolution.

Line-of-sight interpretation

InSAR measures displacement along the radar line-of-sight only. A vertical-only event (subsidence) is partially sampled; a horizontal-only event (strike-slip faulting) differs between ascending and descending tracks. Combine ascending and descending passes to decompose into vertical and east-west components where needed.

Reliability checks

  • Coherence. Exclude low-coherence pixels; trust outputs only above roughly 0.3–0.4 depending on scenario.
  • Reference point. All InSAR velocities are relative. Pick a stable reference and report it.
  • Atmospheric residuals. Troposphere and ionosphere add noise. Weather-model corrections (GACOS, ERA-5) help.
