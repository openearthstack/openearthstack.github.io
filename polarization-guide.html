Polarization Guide.

SAR transmits and receives on a chosen polarisation. The channel you pick changes what the scene tells you.

Single-pol channels

VV
Vertical transmit, vertical receive. Dominant channel for bare soil, agriculture, and urban surfaces. Standard over land.
VH
Vertical transmit, horizontal receive. Cross-polarised returns are weaker but highly sensitive to volume scattering, making VH valuable for vegetation biomass and forest monitoring.
HH
Horizontal transmit, horizontal receive. Strong response over ice and some flood-water surfaces. Widely used in cryosphere and maritime applications.
HV
Horizontal transmit, vertical receive. Cross-polarised. Similar interpretation to VH.

Dual-polarisation

Two channels acquired together: commonly VV+VH (the Sentinel-1 IW default) or HH+HV. The ratio between co-pol and cross-pol is itself informative — it highlights vegetation, helps separate urban from bare surfaces, and feeds most operational classifiers.

Quad-polarisation

All four channels (HH, HV, VH, VV). Enables polarimetric decompositions such as Pauli, Freeman-Durden, and Cloude-Pottier, but at the cost of a much narrower swath. Not available on Sentinel-1; ALOS-2, RADARSAT-2, and some commercial sensors offer it.

Which polarisation do I use?

  • Flood mapping: VV for open water; VH helps separate flooded vegetation.
  • Agriculture and forest: VV+VH, with VH often the stronger predictor.
  • Sea ice and oil spills: HH preferred where available; VV on Sentinel-1.
  • InSAR: Co-pol (VV or HH) for best coherence.
