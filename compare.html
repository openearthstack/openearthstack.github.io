Planet operates the largest optical EO constellation and sells imagery plus analytics through a SaaS platform. OpenEarthStack addresses what optical imagery cannot, persistent all-weather monitoring through cloud, at night, and under smoke, using SAR.

Maxar sells very-high-resolution optical imagery and mapping services. OpenEarthStack adds the weather-independent layer, continuous SAR-based monitoring that keeps working when optical coverage is blocked.

Google Earth Engine is a cloud-based geospatial analysis platform requiring continuous internet and a Google account. OpenEarthStack ships models and pipelines that run fully offline on 8 GB of RAM, same capability class, without the cloud dependency.

Geospatial Foundation Models

Foundation Models

OpenEarthStack vs Prithvi (IBM and NASA)

Prithvi is a foundation model for optical Earth observation trained primarily on Harmonized Landsat-Sentinel data. OpenEarthStack is SAR-native, with benchmarks explicitly covering underrepresented geographies where most public EO models underperform.

Foundation Models

OpenEarthStack vs Clay Foundation

Clay is an open geospatial foundation model with broad modality coverage. OpenEarthStack specialises in SAR and ships a full stack, datasets, fine-tuned models, and edge inference, rather than a general-purpose embedding model alone.

Foundation Models

OpenEarthStack vs SatlasPretrain

SatlasPretrain offers large-scale pretrained EO models from AI2. OpenEarthStack focuses on operational deployment, with per-context benchmarks, edge-runnable checkpoints, and a documented pipeline from raw acquisition to inference.

Traditional SAR Tools

Traditional Tools

OpenEarthStack vs SNAP

ESA's SNAP is free desktop software for manual SAR preprocessing and analysis. OpenEarthStack automates the pipeline from raw Sentinel-1 to analysis-ready output with AI interpretation, removing the manual steps that make SNAP workflows hard to scale.

Traditional Tools

OpenEarthStack vs GMTSAR / ISCE2

GMTSAR and ISCE2 are community-maintained InSAR toolkits used in academic and research workflows. OpenEarthStack wraps and extends these primitives with foundation models and edge inference, so a team does not need an in-house remote-sensing PhD to ship SAR in production.

Methodology

OpenEarthStack vs Manual SAR Analysis

Manual SAR analysis depends on the experience of whoever is reading the imagery. OpenEarthStack applies the same scrutiny to every scene and returns results in seconds instead of hours, freeing experienced analysts for the judgment calls that actually require them.

Methodology Comparisons

Approach over product.

Optical vs SAR for Persistent Monitoring

Optical imagery fails through cloud cover, at night, and under smoke or haze. SAR sees through all of it. For tropical regions, monsoon seasons, and operational contexts that require continuity, SAR is the only sensor that delivers every revisit.

Sentinel-1 C-band vs Commercial X-band SAR

Commercial X-band SAR offers sub-metre resolution at significant cost per scene. Sentinel-1 C-band provides free global coverage every 6 to 12 days at moderate resolution, sufficient for most large-area monitoring workloads when paired with strong models.

Foundation Models vs Task-Specific Models

Task-specific SAR models require large labelled datasets per task and often transfer poorly across regions. Foundation models pre-trained on diverse SAR data adapt to new tasks with far less labelled data and carry per-context benchmarks that tell you where they work.

Cloud Inference vs Edge Deployment

Cloud inference requires bandwidth, connectivity, and data-residency trade-offs. Edge inference runs locally on consumer hardware, essential for field operations, air-gapped environments, and regions without reliable cloud access.

Interoperable Stack vs Proprietary Stack

Proprietary stacks lock teams into a vendor's roadmap and formats. Interoperable stacks speak standard interfaces, ship with documented behaviour, and integrate alongside the tools teams already run.
